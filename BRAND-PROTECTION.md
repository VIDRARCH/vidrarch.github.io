# VIDRARCH Brand Protection Plan

This is a practical launch sequence, not legal advice.

## Phase 0 — Do this before announcing the brand

- Claim the GitHub username `vidrarch`.
- Register the primary domain and defensive domains you care about.
- Turn on registrar lock, auto-renew, WHOIS/RDAP privacy or redaction where offered, DNSSEC where supported, and strong MFA/passkeys.
- Claim the most important matching social handles.
- Save dated evidence of the first public use of VIDRARCH in connection with your portfolio/services.

## Phase 1 — Trademark protection

- Search the USPTO database and broader web/state/common-law sources for exact and confusingly similar marks.
- Prioritize the **word mark VIDRARCH** because it protects the name independent of a particular logo treatment.
- If the logo remains stable and materially important, consider a separate design-mark application.
- Use **VIDRARCH™** while claiming the mark. Do not use ® until a federal registration issues for the relevant goods/services.
- For the safest filing strategy, use a U.S.-licensed trademark attorney to assess classes, identification of goods/services, filing basis, and likelihood-of-confusion risk.

## Phase 2 — Repository and account hardening

- Keep the repository private until the GitHub handle/domain/social names are secured.
- Require a passkey or hardware-backed MFA on GitHub and the domain registrar.
- Enable GitHub rulesets/branch protection for `main`.
- Require pull requests for changes once the site is public.
- Enable secret scanning and push protection where available.
- Enable Dependabot for GitHub Actions.
- Keep production-sensitive information out of the repository; follow `PUBLIC-SAFETY-CHECKLIST.md`.

## Phase 3 — Monitor

- Set search alerts for `VIDRARCH` and close variants.
- Periodically re-check GitHub/social handles and domain variants.
- Keep dated screenshots, invoices, domain registrations, repository history, and published pages showing your use of the mark.

## Copyright note

Do not rely on copyright to protect the **name**. Names and short phrases are not protected by U.S. copyright law. The logo is AI-assisted, so copyrightability can depend on the extent of human-authored expressive contribution and modification. Trademark protection is the primary protection strategy for the brand identity.
